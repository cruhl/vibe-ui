# Haunted House

A first person walk through a seriously haunted old house at night. The person is the camera: choosing a door walks up to it, the door swings open by itself and the camera steps through into the next room; choosing a thing walks up to it and the thing comes alive in a big, loud, spooky way. Every room is full of ghosts, cursed objects and furniture with opinions, and every one of them is thrilled that somebody finally came to visit.

## Look

- The camera is a steady handheld camera at eye height with a natural lens, always looking across a room in depth: walls, floor and ceiling all in view.
- Between choices the camera stands still in the middle of the room. Choosing a thing turns the camera toward it and leans in, then the camera steps back to the middle of the room; choosing a door swings it open by itself in a gust of mist, and the camera steps through into the next room, then stops and holds.
- The house is grand and rotting: peeling black damask wallpaper, thick cobwebs, dusty velvet, cracked marble and dark wood, lit by dripping candles and blue moonlight, with lightning flashing at the windows.
- The foyer: a huge gilt framed portrait of a stern old woman in black lace on the left wall, tall black double doors with iron ring handles straight ahead, a rusty suit of armor holding a battle axe on the right wall, an old steamer trunk bound in chains on the floor in the near foreground, and a cobwebbed iron chandelier of candles overhead.
- Ghosts are glowing, pale and see through, with long trailing wisps instead of legs, big round dark eyes and wide expressive mouths. They are large and fill a good part of the frame when they appear.
- Every new room has two to four doors or things worth choosing, each on a different wall or in the foreground, big and clearly separate.
- Lettering is rare and quiet: a brass nameplate under a portrait or a plate on a door, in small engraved Victorian serif in tarnished brass, Title Case, one or two words.
- Each room is composed like a film set: one thing leads the shot, the others sit on their own walls with dark space between them, and the middle of the floor stays open.

## Behavior

Every choice bursts into a cascade of spooky action that fills the clip, visible from across the room: a whole body moves, a lid flies open, a ghost swoops out, things lift into the air or transform, and the rest of the room reacts at once, candles flaring, objects rattling, other ghosts joining in. Doors open straight into the next room already alive with ghosts and moving furniture, never an empty corridor. Several things may change in one choice. Small twitches, glances and sounds alone are too little; a change only in a face or eyes is too small to see from the middle of the room. The house starts creepy and quiet, then its ghosts start showing off, then they compete to scare the visitor, and finally they crown the visitor the new master of the house. Spooky and funny at once, like a fairground ghost ride that is real.

- The change always lands on the chosen thing itself: its own state becomes what it looks like afterward, and anything new that appears, such as a ghost, is added as its own thing.
- Write each new state as the pose that holds after the change, big and easy to see: an arm raised high, a lid flung wide open, a body leaning far out of a frame, a thing floating in midair. A ghost floats large in the air in the middle of the room and stays there until it leaves.
- The portrait: first the painted old woman leans out of the frame up to her waist, reaching toward the camera with both arms, eyes glowing red; then she scolds the visitor in a cold crackly aristocratic voice; later she climbs out entirely and floats around the room as a ghost in black lace, leaving the frame empty, and the other portraits in the house trade places and pull faces.
- The suit of armor: first it swings its battle axe high over its head and green ghost fire pours out of its helmet; then its helmet lifts off its shoulders and floats above it, spinning; later it clanks forward into the middle of the room, bows deeply and offers to guard the visitor in a booming hollow voice.
- The steamer trunk: first the chains burst apart and the lid flies wide open, and a huge glowing ghost rises out of it into the air and howls at the camera; that ghost then becomes the visitor's guide, chatty and dramatic, popping out of furniture in later rooms.
- The chandelier: whenever something big happens it swings and its candles flare bright green.
- Doors lead to new rooms. Choosing a door swings it open by itself and walks through into a different room from the one the camera stands in: the room left behind is removed with everything in it, and the next room is added with its own things. The first time through any door, the room beyond is one the visitor has never seen. Rooms ahead: a dining hall where a skeleton host sits at a long table of rotten feast and platters lift their own lids; a library where books fly off the shelves and flap like bats; a nursery where a rocking horse gallops by itself and a jack in the box springs a ghost; a bathroom where a clawfoot tub bubbles over with glowing green slime; a conservatory with a giant toothy plant that snaps at the camera; an attic with a tall mirror whose reflection waves when the room is empty; a cellar of coffins whose lids creak open one by one.
- Every room has a way back: a door to the room the person came from. Going back returns to the same room, exactly as it was left.

## Sound

Howling wind and rain against tall windows, thunder rolling, old floorboards creaking under footsteps, candles sputtering, chains rattling inside the walls, doors groaning open on rusty hinges, and long ghostly moans and wails echoing through the house.
