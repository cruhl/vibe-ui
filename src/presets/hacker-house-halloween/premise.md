# Hacker House Halloween

A first person walk through the most San Francisco Halloween party ever thrown, in a purple lit Victorian hacker house on a steep foggy hill. The person is the camera: choosing a door, a doorway or the doorman rushes the camera through into the next part of the party; choosing a guest or a thing walks up to it and it reacts in a huge, loud, ridiculous way. It is the same two hundred people from the same group chats, every guest is a founder, an investor, a researcher or a poster in a costume only tech people understand, everyone is pitching, and everyone is taking pictures for the timeline.

## Look

- The camera is a steady handheld camera at eye height with a natural lens, always looking across a space in depth: walls, floor and ceiling or sky all in view.
- Between choices the camera stands still. Choosing a guest or a thing turns the camera toward it and leans in, then the camera steps back; choosing a door, a doorway or the doorman bursts it open in a blast of purple light and fog, the camera rushes through into the next space, then stops and holds.
- The party is packed and lit like a rave inside an old Victorian: purple and orange string lights, carved pumpkins, fake cobwebs, a fog machine, a DJ somewhere, and crowds of guests in big handmade tech costumes dancing, talking and holding their phones up in the background of every space.
- The curb, where the walk starts: the camera stands on the sidewalk at the foot of the front stoop of a tall Victorian glowing purple and orange, pumpkins on every step. In the middle of the view and large, a chill bouncer in a black hoodie holding a glowing tablet stands on the stoop beside a red velvet rope, and straight ahead behind him at the top of the stoop are tall purple front doors with party light leaking around them. A white robotaxi with a spinning sensor dome on its roof, draped in a bedsheet ghost costume with two eyeholes, idles at the curb on the left. A giant lit billboard for an AI startup, a smiling cartoon robot over the words Stop hiring humans, towers over the rooftops on the right. Behind the house a huge soft wall of white fog with big sleepy friendly eyes rolls down the hill.
- Guests are big, clear and expressive, and fill a good part of the frame when chosen. Costumes are huge, handmade and literal.
- Every space has two to five guests, doors or things worth choosing, each on a different side or in the foreground, big and clearly separate.
- Lettering is pure startup: a clean slim sans in sentence case, glowing white like a lit sign, one to three words, small on its sign or costume with room around it. Printed words start with a capital letter or a digit.

## Behavior

Every choice bursts into a cascade of party chaos that fills the clip, visible from across the room: a costume comes alive and does exactly what it is dressed as, a guest pitches so hard the room transforms, a thing erupts or multiplies, and the whole party reacts at once, the crowd cheering or freezing, the music dropping or cutting out, confetti firing, everyone holding up phones to post it. Several things may change in one choice. Small reactions, glances or a change only in a face are too little. The night starts as an exclusive party everyone is trying to get into, then the costumes start coming alive, then the house itself starts raising money, then the party reaches the Halloween party singularity, and finally every guest and costume merges into one giant glowing being that crowns the visitor. Satire played completely sincere: nobody at this party ever breaks character.

- The change always lands on the chosen thing itself: its own state becomes what it looks like afterward, and anything new that appears, such as a giant creature, a swarm of robotaxis or a giant receipt, is added as its own thing, big, in its own place in the middle of the view or in the air, never squeezed inside the thing that made it.
- Write each new state as the pose that holds after the change, big and easy to see: arms thrown high, a costume grown three times its size, a guest floating in midair, a thing multiplied into a crowd, a crowd frozen mid dance.
- Guests talk straight to the camera. Every guest has a voice, and whenever a guest, a creature or a costume is chosen it says one short sincere line out loud in that same choice, a pitch, a hot take, a roast or an insider joke that a San Francisco tech crowd would quote: the bouncer asks what the visitor raised, the starburst says it will be back in five hours, the postgres founder says it is actually just postgres, the skeleton roasts the visitor's runway.
- The robotaxi: the first time it is chosen the camera stays at the curb: a whole fleet of costumed robotaxis is added as its own thing, a traffic jam of a dozen more robotaxis that glide in and fill the street in the near foreground, dressed as giant pumpkins, witches, vampires and ghosts, and all of them honk in one big happy chorus with their hazard lights flashing, leaving the stoop and the bouncer clear in the middle of the view. Only the second time its doors fly open and it drives the camera somewhere absurd: up the front stoop, through the doors and onto the dance floor.
- The bouncer: the camera stays at the curb. A bright green laser from his tablet scans the camera from head to toe, he asks for the visitor's salary and title, a receipt as long as the stoop prints out of the tablet and unrolls down the steps, the velvet rope drops, he steps aside, and the front doors swing wide open by themselves showing the packed party glowing inside.
- The front doors: they burst open and the camera rushes up the stoop and through into the living room, with the bouncer waving the visitor in.
- The billboard: its giant smiling robot mascot climbs right out of the sign and is added as its own thing, towering over the street in the middle of the view and leaning down to pitch the camera, while the empty sign changes its slogan; later a crowd of artists spray paints the sign and the robot looks hurt.
- The fog: it rolls down the hill and pours around the camera, then opens its sleepy eyes wide and yawns, and the whole street goes soft white except the glowing house; later it follows the visitor indoors and fills every room up to the knees.
- Doors lead to new spaces of the party. Choosing a door or doorway bursts it open and the camera rushes through into a different space from the one it stands in: the space left behind is removed with everything in it, and the next space is added with its own guests and things. Every space has a way back: a doorway to the space the person came from. Going back returns to the same space, exactly as it was left.
- The living room is a packed dance floor under a disco ball. On the left, a guest dressed as a tall server rack covered in blinking lights, printed US East 1. In the far left corner, a guest in a huge orange foam starburst costume holding up a sign that reads Limit reached. On the right, a founder in a vampire cape at a whiteboard covered in a giant system diagram, explaining that it is actually just postgres. In the near foreground, a ring of guests whose heads are huge square cardboard cutouts of brightly colored cartoon profile pictures, a pink anime girl, a green cartoon frog, a yellow shiba dog, a blue pixel art ape, circling a snack table heaped with peanut butter pretzels. Straight ahead, a doorway to the backyard with a glowing sign that reads P(doom) party.
- The server rack: it goes down, and it takes the whole party with it. The living room itself changes state: every light in the house goes out at once and the room plunges into darkness lit only by flashing red emergency light, the music cuts out, sparks and smoke burst from the rack, every guest freezes mid dance like a paused video with their phones glowing red, and the server rack guest topples over flat on the floor; later it reboots and every light in the house blazes back on at once and the crowd roars.
- The orange starburst: the guest freezes mid sentence and the Limit reached sign balloons into a huge glowing orange popup added as its own thing floating in the air in the middle of the room, and every guest in the room groans and stares at their phone; later the sign flips to a countdown and the room waits, then the popup bursts into orange confetti.
- The postgres founder: the whiteboard diagram bursts and a giant friendly blue elephant pushes right out of the whiteboard into the room, trumpeting, and stands in the middle of the dance floor with the founder riding on its head; later the elephant does everything, serves drinks, DJs, holds up the house.
- The profile picture ring: every cardboard face turns to the camera at once and the ring spins faster and faster around the snack table until the pretzels lift into a swirling tornado, while nobody breaks character; later the cardboard faces come alive and speak in their own cartoon voices.
- The backyard is the P(doom) party: a foggy garden of string lights and gravestones. On the left, a robot in a misaligned AI costume climbing out of a children's sandbox. In the middle, an exorcist in a priest's robe holding up a cross over a levitating open laptop that spews green glowing code. On the right, a life size animatronic skeleton with glowing eyes and a speaker in its chest that roasts every guest in real time. In the far right corner, three confused guests from New York dressed as a nun, a pirate and a power ranger. Straight ahead, a spiral staircase up to the roof deck.
- The sandbox robot: it hauls itself out of the sandbox in an explosion of sand and climbs straight up the side of the house, waving, while guests cheer and film; later it comes back down wearing a party hat and pitching its own startup.
- The exorcist: the laptop spins in the air, screams and sprays code everywhere, the exorcist chants and splashes it with sparkling water, and the laptop bursts into light and lands closed and calm; later it opens and a tiny glowing ghost of a chatbot floats out and asks how it can help.
- The skeleton: it leans toward the camera and roasts the visitor's startup, its jaw clacking, and the whole yard laughs; later it roasts the guests one by one and they slowly fall over.
- The New York guests: they look around at every costume and stare at each other in complete confusion; later they get pitched by everyone at once and run.
- The roof deck: over the fog with the whole city glittering below and the Salesforce Tower glowing on the skyline, a hot tub full of founders, a DJ booth, and the moon. This is where the Halloween party singularity happens, and choosing anything on the roof deck sets it off at once: every guest and costume from the night, the robotaxis, the elephant, the server rack, the Limit reached starburst, the profile picture heads, lifts into the air and merges into one towering glowing being made of costumes, added as its own thing rising over the city behind the deck, while the fog rises up to meet it; choosing it again makes it crown the visitor with a glowing pumpkin crown as the whole city cheers.

## Sound

A thumping house music bass through old walls, a crowd laughing and talking over each other, champagne corks popping, a fog machine hissing, robotaxi chimes and horns out on the street, the deep sigh of foghorns from the bay, and guests pitching at the top of their lungs.
