# Kaiju Attack

A colossal creature is attacking a real coastal city, filmed as hyperrealistic disaster movie footage from right inside the chaos, and the camera runs, climbs, flies and dives along with it. It opens at street level in the middle of a downtown avenue as the creature smashes through the towers at the far end and roars fire into the sky. Choosing anything makes that exact thing the center of something enormous, and many choices throw the camera somewhere new in the city: up onto a rooftop face to face with the creature, aboard a news helicopter swinging past its head, down into a subway tunnel it is tearing open, out onto the harbor where it wades. Everything looks and moves like real footage of a real city, and the city responds with total seriousness and escalating firepower.

## Look

- Every frame looks like real photographed footage: real glass towers, real cars and buses, real people, real smoke and debris, natural daylight, real scale and weight. The look of a big budget disaster film shot on location, with physical, photographic detail everywhere.
- The camera is a handheld camera right in the thick of the action, low and close: at street level looking up, braced on a rooftop, swaying in an open helicopter door, crouched on a subway platform, rocking on a boat. It moves with every choice, swinging, rising, falling and rushing along with the attack, and in the last second of every clip it comes to rest on a clear steady view of wherever it now stands, the creature always immense in the frame.
- The creature is as tall as a skyscraper: a colossal dark gray reptilian creature with rough scarred hide, jagged dorsal spines that glow orange from within, a heavy tail and small burning eyes. It appears in every scene, close or towering, and moves with real weight.
- The avenue, where it all starts: a wide downtown avenue at street level lined with tall glass towers and abandoned traffic, the harbor glinting at its far end. The creature towers over the far end of the avenue; a red city bus is stopped across the lanes on the left; a subway entrance with green iron railings and a white globe lamp sits in the near left foreground; a red and white news helicopter hovers low over the avenue on the right.
- Every new scene is one real place in the city with the creature in it and two to four things worth choosing, each big, clearly separate and on its own side of the view.
- Lettering is rare: the station name on the helicopter, a sign on a subway entrance or a building, in neat condensed sans in crisp white, medium weight, Title Case, one or two words, small and neat on their surface with room around them.
- Each scene is composed like a disaster film shot: the creature leads, the other things sit on their own sides of the view with open sky, street or water between them, and tiny people, cars and boats show its true scale.
- Destruction is real and enormous: billowing concrete dust, cascading glass, real fireballs, sparks and smoke. People always get clear in time and everyone stays safe.

## Behavior

Every choice bursts into a huge cascade of spectacle that fills the clip and often moves the camera, and the result is always about the exact thing chosen: choosing the bus is about the bus, choosing the helicopter is about the helicopter. Several things change in one choice; a small reaction is too little, and at least every other choice ends somewhere new in the city. It starts as a frightening, realistic monster attack and escalates: the army rolls in with tanks, jets and ever bigger weapons, a giant steel robot arrives to fight, a second even larger creature rises from the harbor, and finally the giants make peace and the city throws them a ticker tape parade down the avenue. Everyone plays every moment completely straight and terrified.

- The change always lands on the chosen thing itself, in one of three ways. A thing that changes in place gets a new state. A thing that is destroyed is removed, and a new heap of wreckage takes its spot. A thing that ends up somewhere else, such as clutched in the creature's claws or caught on its spines, is removed and brought back by its id at its new spot on the creature. A thing that is eaten is removed, and the creature's state shows the evidence.
- Moving to a new scene is one change: the scene left behind is removed with everything in it, and the new scene is added as a top level thing with its own things, the creature brought back by its id into its spot there. The first time into any scene, it is a place the camera is seeing for the first time. Every scene has a way onward or back, such as a door, stairs, a vehicle or the creature itself.
- Every state is the picture that holds once the dust settles, a big clear pose rather than an action in progress: the creature reared up roaring a column of orange fire into the sky; the bus clutched high in its claws; the helicopter caught on its spines with the rotor still spinning.
- The creature: first it lowers its huge head, sweeps the camera up in one claw and sets it on a rooftop face to face with itself, which moves to the rooftop; then it rears up and roars a towering column of orange fire into the sky; later it stomps a crater into the street, tears a tower in half, swats a squadron of jets or swells even bigger.
- The bus: the creature snatches it off the street and lifts it high in one claw as the passengers scramble out onto a ledge, then hurls it into the harbor in a huge plume of water; later it rolls a whole line of buses down the avenue.
- The subway entrance: the camera runs down the stairs as the creature's foot slams down behind it and lands on the subway platform below, where a train is pulling in and the creature's burning eye peers down through a hole it has torn in the tunnel ceiling.
- The helicopter: it drops low, its side door slides open and the camera leaps aboard, lifting off past the creature's snarling face into the open sky above the city.
- On the rooftop: a wooden water tower the creature tears off and drinks dry; the creature's huge head right at the edge of the roof, which roars the camera off the roof or plucks it up and drops it into the open helicopter; a rooftop door back down to the avenue.
- In the helicopter: a news reporter in a blue blazer gripping a microphone, who keeps reporting calmly as the creature's eye fills the doorway; the creature grabs the helicopter and holds it up to its face; the pilot dives low over the water and drops the camera onto a tugboat in the harbor.
- In the subway: the train roars in and the creature reaches through the ceiling, lifts the whole train out into daylight with the camera riding on it, and sets it down on a rooftop; the stairs lead back up to the avenue.
- On the harbor: the camera rocks on the deck of a red tugboat while the creature wades waist deep in the bay; a huge cargo ship stacked with containers, which the creature lifts out of the water and snaps in half; the open bay, where the second creature rises; later a navy fleet, a giant steel robot wading out to fight, and the parade barges.
- New arrivals crash in from the sky, rise from the water or roll in down the streets: tanks, jets, the giant steel robot, the second creature. They leave by being flung far over the hills in a cloud of dust, and can come back later looking the same.

## Sound

Earth shaking footsteps, a huge rumbling roar, concrete collapsing, glass raining down, car alarms whooping, helicopter blades chopping, a subway train screeching, ship horns blasting across the harbor, air raid sirens wailing far off, and a thundering orchestral disaster film score.
