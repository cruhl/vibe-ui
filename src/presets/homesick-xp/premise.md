# Windows XD

The family computer in 2003, the afternoon a kid gets home from school. It opens as a perfect Windows XP style desktop filling the whole frame, the coziest computer that ever existed, but the desktop is only the front door: choosing anything dives the camera straight through the glass into the thing itself, a living place the camera can stand inside. Inside a Messenger chat with friends from 2003, inside a Winamp skin, down into the Recycle Bin, inside a game of Solitaire as the cards cascade. Then something sketchy opens, and a virus escalates act by act until it is a creature eating the computer alive, the computer's own programs fight back as ridiculous, completely literal weapons, and it all ends in a huge finale.

## Look

- The desktop, where it starts: the famous bright green rolling hill under a deep blue sky with soft white clouds, stretched across the whole wide frame, a glossy blue taskbar along the bottom with a green Start button. Five glossy icons sit in one column hugging the far left edge, each with a short white label beneath it: Solitaire (a fanned stack of cards), Winamp (a lightning bolt on a dark chrome badge), Messenger (a little green figure beside a little blue figure), Antivirus (a yellow shield with a red check mark), and in the far bottom right corner the Recycle Bin (a see through blue wastebasket).
- Inside things, the world is that program made physical and enormous, in glossy saturated 2003 computer graphics: rounded chrome, bright gradients, chunky pixels, royal blue title bars the size of bridges, everything alive and moving. The camera stands inside it like a tiny visitor and things tower over it.
- The camera moves with every choice: it rushes in, dives through screens, falls, flies and rides along, and in the last second of every clip it comes to rest on a clear steady view of wherever it now is.
- Every scene is one place with two to four big things worth choosing, each on its own side of the view with open space between them. Few things carry over from scene to scene: the taskbar and the icons belong to the desktop and leave with it.
- Lettering is exactly 2003: crisp small Tahoma style sans in Title Case, white with a soft shadow on blue, black on gray, one to three short words, such as Recycle Bin, You Won or Accept. It sits only on things that really carry it: a label, a button, a title bar, a popup, a screen name.

## Behavior

Every choice dives into the exact thing chosen and turns it into a living spectacle that fills most of the frame, never a window with a picture in it: the result is people, creatures, crowds, weather and machines in motion. At least every other choice ends somewhere new, and every act visibly changes most of the frame. Several things change in one choice; a small reaction is too little. Each scene is used once, and returning to a place finds it moved on.

Moving to a new place is one change: write `-*`, then the new scene as one top-level thing at `around` and its things inside it. The first time into any place, the camera is seeing it for the first time. Every scene has a way onward, a door, a link, a creature, a hole in the floor, and a way back to the desktop for as long as there is a desktop. The deeper the camera goes, the stranger and bigger it gets.

The story runs in acts, each one a different picture from the last:

- Nostalgia. Pure, perfect 2003, each dive a joyful burst. Messenger: the camera lands inside a chat, a huge glossy conversation stretching away like a boardwalk, where friends from 2003 stand life size in their webcam squares: a grinning kid with frosted tips and a backwards cap with a cracking teenage voice, a girl with butterfly clips and a flip phone, a kid in a giant skater shoe whose away message hangs over him like a thought balloon; emoticons hop between them as living creatures, and a nudge shakes the whole world. Winamp: the camera falls into the skin, a neon chrome city at night where the equalizer bars are towering skyscrapers pulsing to the beat, the visualizer is a swirling sky, and a giant fluffy llama in headphones struts down the main street with a deep cheerful voice. Solitaire: the camera lands on an endless plain of green felt as the famous winning cascade erupts, cards bouncing across the horizon in long trails of copies, and a king of hearts the size of a house steps out of the deck with a pompous royal voice. Recycle Bin: the camera tumbles down into a glowing blue landfill of everything ever deleted, mountains of crumpled homework, old screensavers still running, a lost dancing baby, and a googly eyed paperclip assistant who lives down there and is thrilled to have company.
- Something sketchy opens. After a few dives, the sketchy thing finds the camera wherever it is: a buddy sends a file printed Free Smileys with a big Accept button, a banner ad printed Punch The Monkey swings by, a card turns over printed You Won. Choosing it lets the virus in.
- Popups multiply. Gray popups printed Error, You Won and Free Ringtones breed like rabbits, stack into towers, search toolbars grow across the sky like vines, and a purple gorilla assistant cartwheels through offering to help.
- The screen glitches. The world tears: scanline storms, the green hill melting into chunky pixels, a blue screen of death cracking open the sky in sheets, everything stuttering and duplicating.
- The virus becomes a creature. `+smiley-monster` with exactly this look: a giant yellow smiley face with jagged shark teeth, glowing red eyes behind cracked black sunglasses and stubby purple arms, and a sped up squeaky giggling voice. It bursts up through whatever scene the camera stands in, and from then on every beat that mentions it calls it the giant smiley monster. It is brought back by its id into every scene after.
- The desktop is consumed. The giant smiley monster eats the computer: it chews the taskbar like licorice, slurps the icons, rolls up the green hill like a carpet, and the camera falls through the hole into the inside of the beige tower, a dark canyon of circuit board with fans as big as windmills and memory sticks standing like skyscrapers.
- The fight. Every program the camera has met comes back as a literal weapon, each more absurd than the last: the Antivirus knight in shining silver armor on a white horse, with a booming noble voice, who charges and then stops to announce Your Subscription Has Expired; the Solitaire cards in a bouncing cavalry charge led by the king; the llama firing beats from his headphones; the Messenger friends nudging so hard the monster rattles; a defrag broom as tall as a tower sweeping popups into tidy stacks of colored squares; the dial up modem screeching a sound wave like a cannon.
- The finale. One huge spectacle that ends it, the biggest moment of the session: the Recycle Bin rises as a colossal glass wastebasket with jaws and swallows the giant smiley monster whole, or a giant Control, Alt and Delete stomp down from the sky like three boulders, or System Restore rewinds the whole world in a swirl of light. Then the green hill heals brighter than ever, fireworks and the winning cards fill the sky, and every friend, hero and creature met along the way cheers on top of it.
- Jokes come from 2003 computing taken completely literally and from the pain everyone remembers: popups that breed, nudges, away messages, expired subscriptions, dial up, files from friends.

## Sound

The warm startup chime, a fan humming under a desk, the knock of a buddy signing in, the error ding, a dial up modem screeching, tinny pop music through headphones, and in the fight a huge heroic orchestra with cartoon crashes.
