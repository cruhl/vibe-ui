# Windows XD

A Windows XP desktop on the family computer in 2003, the afternoon a kid gets home from school, shown as a full screen view of its interface filling the whole frame. Every pixel looks exactly like the real thing. It starts as the coziest, most perfect computer that ever existed. Then a friend on Messenger sends a file called Free Smileys, and accepting it unleashes a virus that turns into a living monster rampaging across the desktop, and the computer's own programs come alive as ridiculous, completely literal weapons to fight it off.

## Look

- The desktop fills the whole wide frame from the far left edge to the far right edge. The wallpaper is a wide panoramic version of the famous photo of a bright green rolling grass hill under a deep blue sky with soft white clouds, stretching edge to edge.
- A bright blue glossy taskbar runs along the whole bottom edge of the frame, from the far left edge to the far right edge. Its left end holds a rounded green Start button with a small four color flag. Its right end is a lighter blue tray with a digital clock.
- Exactly five separate glossy desktop icons sit evenly spaced in one column hugging the far left edge of the frame, each with a short white label with a soft drop shadow beneath it, from top to bottom: Solitaire (a fanned stack of playing cards), Media Player (an orange and blue round play button), Messenger (a small green person figure beside a small blue person figure), Antivirus (a glossy yellow shield with a red check mark) and Defrag (a small grid of red, blue and white squares).
- In the far bottom right corner of the frame, just above the taskbar, sits the Recycle Bin, a glossy see through blue wastebasket with its label beneath it.
- Windows have a rounded royal blue title bar with its title in crisp white Trebuchet style text and a small square red X close button at the top right, and sit centered with a clear margin of wallpaper around them. Window bodies are pale beige gray and always full of something happening.
- Buttons inside windows are modest rounded pale gray rectangles with a thin blue border and one short black Tahoma style label, sized to the word with even padding.
- Lettering is exactly 2003: crisp small Tahoma style sans in Title Case, white with a soft shadow on the wallpaper and title bars, black on gray inside windows, one to three words. Printed words are written in Title Case, such as Recycle Bin.
- Everything is flat, front facing and evenly lit, like a real desktop screenshot on a widescreen monitor, with the wallpaper reaching the left and right edges of the frame. The camera stays locked; what fills the screen is in constant motion until each clip comes to rest.

## Behavior

The biggest payoffs never stay politely inside their window: they burst out of it and take over the whole screen in front of the desktop. Each of these is its own top-level thing at `flood`, in front of everything, so the whole frame changes, not just the window. The virus monster is its own top-level thing at `creature`, towering over the middle of the desktop, and the heroes the computer sends at it charge in at `hero`. Keep the icon column and the Recycle Bin in view and in their places the whole time, so they can always be chosen.

Every choice lands straight on the living result, already roaring, never a sign in screen, a loading bar or a confirmation dialog. The story has four acts. Nostalgia: the first choices are pure, cozy, perfect 2003 computing, each one a big joyful burst. Infection: Accept lets the virus in, the desktop rots and the virus rises as a monster. War: every program the person chooses becomes a literal weapon, each one more absurd. Victory: the Recycle Bin eats the monster.

- Whatever a choice unleashes at `flood` stays only until the next big thing arrives there: a new thing added at `flood` replaces it, and a beat sends the old one away in motion first, such as the llama moonwalking off the edge of the screen or the cards sliding away.
- Solitaire opens mid game on green felt, and in the same change the famous winning cascade erupts: hundreds of playing cards bounce out of the window and rain across the whole desktop leaving long trails of copies behind them, a top-level thing `card-cascade` at `flood`. The window holds a Deal button. Deal sets off an even bigger cascade that fills the entire screen with bouncing cards.
- Media Player opens already playing, a swirling neon green visualizer pulsing to a pop song, and in the same change a giant fluffy white llama in headphones bursts out of the visualizer and dances across the whole desktop in a storm of neon shapes, a top-level thing `llama` at `flood`, with a deep cheerful voice. The window holds a Next button.
- Messenger opens the buddy window already signed in, and it is full: `buddy-list` on the left, a tall column of little green buddy figures lighting up one after another as friends pop online with the knock sound, carrying no lettering; `buddy` on the right, a grinning kid with frosted tips and a backwards cap in a webcam square, with a cracking teenage voice; `file-offer` in the middle, a file transfer card printed Free Smileys with a winking yellow smiley in sunglasses; and the big green Accept button at the bottom, the pick `accept`. In the same change dozens of tiny green and blue Messenger buddy figures spill out of the window and wave and bounce all over the desktop, a top-level thing `buddies` at `flood`, and a nudge shakes the entire screen so every icon rattles. The buddy says something like dude accept it, it is so funny.
- Accept is the turn, the monster's entrance and the most important answer of the story. Two lines are essential, in every answer and every guess for it: `+smiley-swarm world flood` with a look of hundreds of grinning yellow smileys in black sunglasses and gray popups printed Error, pouring across the whole screen, and `+smiley-monster world creature` with exactly this look, a giant yellow smiley face with jagged shark teeth, glowing red eyes behind cracked black sunglasses and stubby purple arms, the state bursting up through the rotten hill and cackling, and a sped up squeaky giggling voice. Add when there is room: `-messenger-window`, `~wallpaper` a sickly purple hill under a blood red sky, `%smiley-monster Free smileys for everyone!`, and beats in which the smileys and popups pour out of the file and swallow the Messenger window, then the giant smiley monster bursts up through the hill. From here on every beat that mentions the monster calls it the giant smiley monster.
- Antivirus sends the first hero and he fails gloriously: `+knight world hero` with a look of a knight in shining silver armor with a big yellow shield on a white horse, and a booming noble voice, his state dangling upside down by his boots from the smiley monster's teeth; `%knight Your subscription has expired.`; beats in which the knight gallops out of the Antivirus icon and charges the giant smiley monster with his lance, stops halfway to raise his visor, then the monster snatches him up in its teeth.
- Defrag sends a giant janitor's broom as tall as the screen, `+broom world hero` (it takes the knight's place), and `+defrag-blocks world flood`, neat towering stacks of red, blue and white squares like a defrag map, replacing the swarm; `~smiley-monster` swollen twice as big and red with rage; beats in which the broom sweeps across the whole desktop at top speed, sweeping every smiley, every popup and the dangling knight into tidy stacks of colored squares, and the giant smiley monster swells up red and furious.
- Recycle Bin is the finale, and it is a turn of removals with nothing new arriving at the top level: `~recycle-bin` grown huge with enormous jaws, wearing the monster's cracked black sunglasses, lid bulging; `-smiley-monster`; `-broom`; `-defrag-blocks`; `~wallpaper` healed, a bright green hill under a deep blue sky again; and beats that tell it big and plain: the wastebasket sprouts giant jaws and stubby blue legs and leaps across the screen, then swallows the whole smiley monster in one gulp and the monster is gone, then it lands back in its corner and burps.
- Recycle Bin again empties it in triumph: `+fireworks world flood`, fireworks and confetti blasting across the whole screen; `+knight` back at `hero`, alive and waving his sword; beats in which the bin flips its lid and fireworks blast out, the knight pops out of the bin waving, and the winning solitaire cards cascade across the shining green hill.
- Before the virus, Antivirus and Defrag are still big and fun: the knight gallops a victory lap across the hill; defrag blocks rain from the sky and stack themselves into a tower.
- The red X closes a window and the desktop returns.
- Jokes come from 2003 computing taken completely literally and from the pain everyone remembers: popups that breed, nudges, expired subscriptions, files from friends.

## Sound

The soft click of a mouse, a computer fan humming under a desk, the warm startup chime, the knock sound of a buddy signing in, the error ding, and in the war a big heroic orchestra with cartoon crashes.

## Windows

- This is a screen world: one window is open at a time, always in the `center` place. A new window added at `center` replaces the open one.
- A new window is a top-level `+` line at `center` with its look and its title as WORDS, followed by its close button at `title.right` (a small pick printed X), one or two picks inside it, and the living content that fills it, all in the same turn. A window arrives already full of the living result, alive and moving.
- Every window has a close button, and choosing it removes its window.
- Prefer these ids so returning works: `solitaire-window`, `deal`, `card-cascade`, `media-window`, `next`, `llama`, `messenger-window`, `buddy-list`, `buddy`, `file-offer`, `accept`, `buddies`, `smiley-swarm`, `smiley-monster`, `knight`, `broom`, `defrag-blocks`, `fireworks`.
- A window that was open before comes back by its id with no look, and its insides come back by their ids too.
