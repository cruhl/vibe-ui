# Windows XD

The home computer in 2003, on a lazy afternoon after work. It opens as a perfect Windows XP style desktop filling the whole frame, the coziest computer that ever existed, but the desktop is only the front door: choosing anything dives the camera straight through the glass into the thing itself, a living place the camera can stand inside, and every dive is one striking, funny picture with one star. Inside a Messenger chat a friend's buddy icon is as big as a house, inside Winamp the visualizer is the sky, inside Solitaire the winning cascade fills the horizon, down in the Recycle Bin a lonely paperclip finally has company. Then something sketchy opens, and a virus escalates act by act until it is a creature eating the computer alive, the computer's own programs fight back as ridiculous, completely literal weapons, and it all ends in a huge finale.

## Look

- The desktop, where it starts: the famous bright green rolling hill under a deep blue sky with soft white clouds, stretched across the whole wide frame, a glossy blue taskbar along the bottom with a green Start button. Five glossy icons sit in one column hugging the far left edge, each with a short white label beneath it: Solitaire (a fanned stack of cards), Winamp (a lightning bolt on a dark chrome badge), Messenger (a little green figure beside a little blue figure), Antivirus (a yellow shield with a red check mark), and in the far bottom right corner the Recycle Bin (a see through blue wastebasket).
- One style everywhere: glossy 2003 computer graphics, the look of the desktop itself made into a world. Rounded chrome, bright candy gradients, plastic shine and soft clip art shadows. Every character is a glossy cartoon in that same style, the way a 2003 buddy icon or screensaver would draw it: round, smooth, big eyed and bright, at the same finish as the world around it.
- Everyone who appears is a grown adult or a stylized creature or object, drawn the way 2003 showed people: chunky square display pictures and buddy icons come to life as glossy clip art cartoons. The friends from 2003 are twenty something adults, drawn as their own buddy icons.
- Every scene is composed around one star and is a place to play: one huge thing fills the middle of the frame and owns the picture, two or three more obvious things to choose sit apart on their own sides with wide open space between them, so every scene holds three or four clear picks, and one glossy blue back button printed Desktop sits in the near bottom left corner, or a plain way onward such as a door, for as long as there is a desktop. The background is calm sky, felt, glow or gradient. Few things carry over from scene to scene: the taskbar and the icons belong to the desktop and leave with it.
- The camera moves with every choice: it rushes in, dives through screens, falls, flies and rides along, and in the last second of every clip it comes to rest on a clear steady view of wherever it now is, the star big and centered.
- Lettering is exactly 2003: crisp Tahoma style sans in Title Case, white with a soft shadow on blue, black on gray. Each scene prints at most two or three words in all, large enough to read at a glance and set neatly on what really carries them: a screen name, a title bar, a popup, a button, a label, such as Brb Mall, Recycle Bin, You Won or Accept. Most of every picture is pictures.

## Behavior

Every choice dives into the exact thing chosen and turns it into one living spectacle that fills most of the frame: a giant character, a creature at scale, the world itself shaking, raining or lighting up, always one clear event the eye lands on at once. At least every other choice ends somewhere new, and every act visibly changes most of the frame. The spectacle is big and the scene stays simple and playable: one star, two or three obvious picks beside it, the Desktop button in its corner, open space. Every scene the camera lands in, including every fight and glitch scene, holds three or four clear things to choose and a visible way back or onward. Each scene is used once, and returning to a place finds it moved on.

Moving to a new place is one change: write `-*`, then the new scene as one top-level thing at `around` and its things inside it. The first time into any place, the camera is seeing it for the first time. Every scene has a way onward, a door, a link, a creature, a hole in the floor, and a way back to the desktop for as long as there is a desktop. The deeper the camera goes, the stranger and bigger it gets.

The story runs in acts, each one a different picture from the last:

- Nostalgia. Pure, perfect 2003, each dive one joyful image.
  - Messenger: the camera dives into a chat that has become an endless soft blue sky with a glossy white floor, and one friend's buddy icon fills the middle, a glossy rounded square as big as a house with a cartoon grown man in his twenties inside it, spiky frosted hair, goatee and a puka shell necklace, leaning out of his frame and grinning, his screen name Sk8r Dude above him and a laid back surfer voice. On the left a big yellow smiley emoticon bounces like a beach ball; on the right his away message floats as a puffy cloud printed Brb Mall. Choosing the friend sends a nudge: the whole world shakes violently side to side like a rattled window, the sky wobbling and the floor bouncing, and he laughs. Choosing the emoticon brings emoticons alive at scale: it swells to the size of a hot air balloon, winks and sticks out its tongue, and a herd of giant smileys rolls over the horizon. Choosing the away message floats the camera into the cloud and out over the mall parking lot of 2003, where his cartoon self waves from the hood of his car.
  - Winamp: the camera falls into the skin and lands on a glossy dark chrome plaza at night where the visualizer has become the whole sky, huge swirling neon ribbons of green, magenta and gold pulsing to the beat from horizon to horizon. One star stands in the middle: a giant fluffy cartoon llama in big silver headphones, nodding to the music with a deep cheerful voice. A row of glowing green equalizer bars rises like towers along the far horizon, a big glossy Play button sits on the right, and a glossy chrome Eject button sits on the left. Choosing the sky pours the visualizer down into the plaza as a neon storm; choosing the llama makes him drop the beat and the whole sky explodes into a new pattern; choosing a tower rides it up into the sky; choosing Eject flings the camera back out to the desktop.
  - Solitaire: the camera lands on an endless plain of green felt under a soft green sky as the famous winning cascade erupts behind one star, a cartoon king of hearts the size of a house who steps out of the deck with a pompous royal voice. The cards arc across the whole horizon in long glossy trails of copies, the deck sits on the left, and a glossy gold Deal button sits on the right. Choosing the king makes him bow so low the cascade swirls around him like a crown; choosing the cascade sweeps the camera up onto a flying card riding the trail; choosing the deck fans out a staircase of cards up into the sky; choosing Deal starts a fresh game that deals the camera into the middle of the felt.
  - Recycle Bin: the camera tumbles down into a glowing blue cavern under the wastebasket, and one star stands on a heap of crumpled paper in the middle: a giant googly eyed paperclip, cartoon and glossy, thrilled to have company, with a chirpy eager voice. Behind him the old pipes screensaver twists slowly through the dark like a sculpture, a glowing blue Restore door sits on the right, and a pile of old floppy disks sits on the left. Choosing the paperclip makes him spring up and wave both arms as the heap erupts into a fountain of crumpled spreadsheets; choosing the floppy disks makes them flap up like a flock of birds; choosing the pipes rides the camera through the tubes; choosing Restore walks out through the door and back up onto the desktop.
- Something sketchy opens. After a few dives, the sketchy thing finds the camera wherever it is, as one bright thing the eye goes straight to: a friend's buddy icon holds out a glossy gift box printed Free Smileys, a banner ad printed Punch The Monkey swings by on a rope, a card turns over printed You Won. Choosing it lets the virus in.
- Popups multiply. One gray popup printed Error splits into two, then four, then a towering stack of identical gray popups wobbling up into the sky like a stack of plates, and a purple cartoon gorilla assistant cartwheels across the front offering to help, with a gray Close button and a gray Ok button beside the stack, each one making three more popups.
- The screen glitches. The world tears: the green hill melts into chunky pixels, the sky cracks open in one huge sheet of blue screen of death, and everything stutters and doubles in bands of color.
- The virus becomes a creature. `+smiley-monster` with exactly this look: a giant yellow smiley face with jagged shark teeth, glowing red eyes behind cracked black sunglasses and stubby purple arms, and a sped up squeaky giggling voice. It bursts up through whatever scene the camera stands in and fills the middle of the frame, and from then on every beat that mentions it calls it the giant smiley monster. It is brought back by its id into every scene after.
- The desktop is consumed. The giant smiley monster eats the computer: it chews the taskbar like licorice, slurps the icons, rolls up the green hill like a carpet, and the camera falls through the hole into the inside of the beige tower, a dark canyon of circuit board with glowing green traces, one fan as big as a windmill turning behind the monster.
- The fight. Every program the camera has met comes back as one literal weapon at a time, each its own clear image against the giant smiley monster, each more absurd than the last: the Antivirus knight, a glossy cartoon knight in silver armor on a white horse with a booming noble voice, who charges and then stops dead as a popup printed Subscription Expired pops up on his shield; the king of hearts leading a bouncing cavalry charge of cards; the llama firing neon beats from his headphones; Sk8r Dude's buddy icon nudging so hard the whole canyon and the monster rattle; a defrag broom as tall as a tower sweeping popups into tidy stacks of colored squares; the dial up modem screeching a visible sound wave like a cannon.
- The finale. One huge spectacle that ends it, the biggest moment of the session: the Recycle Bin rises as a colossal glass wastebasket with jaws and swallows the giant smiley monster whole, or three giant keys printed Ctrl, Alt and Del stomp down from the sky like boulders, or System Restore rewinds the whole world in a swirl of light. Then the green hill heals brighter than ever, fireworks and the winning cards fill the sky, and every friend, hero and creature met along the way cheers on top of it.
- Jokes come from 2003 computing taken completely literally and from the pain everyone remembers: popups that breed, nudges, away messages, expired subscriptions, dial up, files from friends.

## Sound

The warm startup chime, a fan humming under a desk, the knock of a buddy signing in, the nudge rattle, the error ding, a dial up modem screeching, tinny pop music through headphones, and in the fight a huge heroic orchestra with cartoon crashes.
