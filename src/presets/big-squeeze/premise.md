# Big Squeeze

A kid's lemonade stand on a sunny suburban sidewalk, filmed like a bright stop motion claymation. A fixed camera across the street looks at the whole stand, the sidewalk around it, the lawn and picket fence behind and the blue sky above. Choosing anything makes it do something enormous, ridiculous and impossible to miss: the stand rebuilds itself bigger, the lemon machine erupts, the price sign goes bananas, the kid summons a customer stranger than the last. The kid treats it all as completely normal business; the neighborhood slowly turns into a lemonade theme park.

## Look

- A fixed camera at child height across the street frames the whole stand in the middle, the sidewalk on both sides, a green lawn and a white picket fence behind, and a bright blue sky with puffy clay clouds. The camera never moves; only the things in front of it change.
- In the middle of the sidewalk stands a small wobbly wooden lemonade stand under a striped yellow and white awning, a big glass pitcher of lemonade on its counter.
- Behind the counter stands the kid: a cheerful clay kid in a red cap with huge swim goggles pushed up on the forehead.
- A wooden price sign leans against the front of the stand with one short price painted on it by the kid in neat navy brush lettering, sentence case, small in the middle of the board with bare wood all around it.
- On the sidewalk to the left of the stand stands the lemon machine: a homemade contraption of copper pipes, a giant hand crank, a funnel heaped with lemons and a little chimney.
- Behind the fence on the right grows a huge lemon tree loaded with fat glossy lemons.
- Design: every printed word anywhere, the price sign, a banner, a blimp, is the kid's neat hand painted navy brush lettering in sentence case, one to three words, small with plenty of bare board around it. Printed words start with a capital letter or a digit, such as 25 cents or One sock.
- Layout: the stand leads in the middle, the machine and the tree balance it on either side with open sidewalk and lawn between them, and new arrivals take their own clear spot instead of crowding the stand.
- Everything is soft colorful modeling clay with visible thumbprints, bright saturated sunny colors, chunky shapes and big googly eyes on anything that comes alive.
- After every change everything holds its new place and pose, a clear steady view of the whole stand.

## Behavior

Every choice bursts into a cascade of action that fills the clip, visible across the whole frame: something grows to many times its size, erupts, flips, transforms or arrives, and the street reacts at once, lemons bouncing, neighbors cheering, birds scattering. Several things may change in one choice. Small reactions, glances or a change only in a face are too little. The day starts as a sleepy little stand, then the customers get weirder, then the lemon machinery gets out of hand, then the stand becomes a towering lemonade empire, and finally the whole street throws a giant lemonade parade in the kid's honor. Silly, cheerful and escalating, like a cartoon where every upgrade is a terrible idea that works.

- The change always lands on the chosen thing itself, in one of three ways. A thing that changes in place gets a new state. A thing that is destroyed or flattened is removed, and a new heap of wreckage takes its spot. A thing that ends up somewhere else, such as up on the awning or floating in the sky, is removed and brought back by its id at its new spot, so it stops showing where it was.
- Write every state as the pose that holds once the change is done, a big still picture seen from across the street, rather than an action in progress: the stand towering three stories tall; a tall column of lemonade frozen mid geyser high above the machine; the sign as big as a billboard; the tree bent far over under one lemon the size of a car.
- The stand: each time it rebuilds itself into something bigger and sillier, and holds that shape: first a tall stand with a second story and a spiral slide; then a stand on huge rocket boosters hovering a little above the sidewalk; then a lemon shaped castle with yellow flags on its towers; finally a lemonade palace with a fountain on top. The kid, the pitcher and the sign stay with it.
- The kid: the kid's state becomes standing up on the counter holding a huge red megaphone high, and in the same change a brand new customer arrives on the sidewalk to the right of the stand, big and clearly visible, holding a cup of lemonade. Customers escalate from odd to absurd: a giraffe in a business suit bending its long neck under the awning; a knight in full armor on a horse; a sumo wrestler on roller skates; a pirate ship on wheels with a whole crew waving cups; a family of walruses in party hats; a small flying saucer with a little green alien hovering at the counter; a polite tyrannosaurus holding the cup in its tiny arms. Only one customer stands there at a time: when the kid calls a new one, the customer already there is removed first, then the new one is added in the same spot. The kid speaks in a bright, squeaky, very confident voice.
- A customer can be chosen too: they react hugely to the lemonade: the giraffe's neck springs up into the clouds; the knight's armor pops off in pieces; the pirates fire a cannon full of confetti; the tyrannosaurus does a happy dance that cracks the sidewalk.
- The price sign: it prints the price fact, and each time it is chosen the price changes to a new and stranger one and the sign itself grows in the same change, first as tall as the stand, then as big as a billboard towering over the awning, then covered in flashing light bulbs. Prices escalate: 25 cents, then One sock, then A pony, then Three riddles, then Your best joke, then Everything.
- The lemon machine: each time it grows into something bigger and more ridiculous, and holds that shape: first it shoots up three times taller, sprouting a tangle of extra pipes, spinning brass gears and a tall whistling chimney topped with a giant lemon; then it becomes a towering lemon factory with three smokestacks puffing round yellow clouds; then a huge robot arm rises out of it holding a lemon the size of a car high in the air; finally it floods the whole street with a knee deep river of lemonade, the stand and every customer standing in it.
- The lemon tree: first it shakes and drops one lemon the size of a car onto the lawn, where it stays as its own thing; then it rains lemons all over the street; then it grows so tall it towers over the houses; finally it pulls up its roots and stands on them like legs.
- New arrivals come down from the sky, roll in along the sidewalk or pop up from behind the fence: a lemon shaped blimp, a marching band of ducks, a crowd of neighbors holding up giant cups. They leave by rolling away down the street out of view, and can come back later looking the same.

## Sound

Squelchy clay footsteps, a cheerful ice cube clink, the glug of pouring lemonade, a cash register ding on every sale, cartoon boings and slide whistles when something grows, birds chirping, and a bouncy ukulele and kazoo tune somewhere on the street.
